# 4. From research to patent

> "If we publish the paper at any conference or journal, the rights are gone. They become public." Salman Butt, [29:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1771s)

## What Salman says

He walks through the pipeline used by companies like Microsoft and Google ([28:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1681s)):

1. **Researchers write** a paper on something related to the company's market ([28:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1703s)).
2. **Patentability check, before publishing** ([28:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1720s)). Specialists search the world's patent databases for anything similar. That includes "academic" patents that some professor filed on a whim ([29:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1747s)). Timing matters because publication puts the idea in the public domain.
3. **An IP-and-product team drafts the claims** ([29:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1793s)). It is a rare hybrid skill, like fintech's mix of finance and technology. The team understands both product-building and patent law.
4. **Open review** ([30:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1837s)). The draft goes to the researcher and the relevant product teams until they agree on the *ground facts*.
5. **The trade-secret split** ([31:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1905s)). Product and legal decide which parts stay secret, like an aircraft's black box: you see what goes in and what comes out, but not what happens inside.
6. **Filing** ([31:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1870s)). In-house legal and the external patent attorney do the talking. Researchers and product people attend silently, so the scope doesn't drift.
7. **Publish** ([32:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1939s)). Once there is a filing number, the researcher adds it to the paper and publishes.

In Saudi Arabia, he says, only large companies like Aramco and SABIC run this pipeline. Small and medium companies don't, and most don't consider it ([32:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1963s)).

## The idea, explained

The order of steps is the whole point. **File first, publish second.** A patent requires *novelty*: the invention must not already be public anywhere. Your own conference talk counts as public, so publishing first can destroy your own patent. Some countries offer a short grace period for an inventor's own disclosure, but many do not, so companies treat "file first" as a hard rule.

**Patent or secret?** A patent gives you a monopoly of about 20 years, and in return you must publish exactly how the invention works. A trade secret never expires, but it gives no protection if someone works it out on their own. Companies usually patent what can be reverse-engineered from the product and keep secret what can't, such as a model's training data or tuning.

**Why the hybrid team is rare.** Patent claims are legal text describing technical boundaries. Writing them well needs someone who understands what the product will become *and* how a court will read the words.
