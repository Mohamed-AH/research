# 8. Founders, teams and AI

> "Money isn't the problem. The question is: will this product sell? Who is your customer? Have you talked to them?" Salman Butt, [1:07:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4022s)

## What Salman says

### Are you fit to be a founder? ([1:06:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=3997s))

- Patent holders often lead with "I need X million and I'll change humanity". They haven't asked whether anyone will buy it ([1:06:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4014s)).
- KnowledgeX's second product, which he calls "Core" (spelt **KORE** on the site), uses **psychometric assessments** to test founder fit ([1:07:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4044s)). "We didn't invent the tests; we invented a different way of analysing them."
- It looks for three personas: the **Engineer** (builds the product), the **Hustler** (sells, hears "no" and keeps going) and the **Artist** ([1:08:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4107s)). He says research shows that having all three in the founding team raises the odds of success from 40% to 55% ([1:08:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4091s)).
- It also maps **team synergy**, meaning where clashes are likely. It has been used with corporate teams and a government team reporting to a deputy minister ([1:09:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4145s)).

### Does AI make research less valuable? ([1:10:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4228s))

- **Partly**, he says. AI is strong at finding and analysing *published* sources ([1:10:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4259s)).
- **But research is more than that.** His example is a neurosurgeon's patented brain-biopsy guide. It sets the needle's angle and depth before drilling, where the old method meant positioning by hand and re-drilling if the angle was wrong ([1:11:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4280s)). AI could not have invented that, because it only knows what is published ([1:13:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4417s)).
- **Core thinking stays human**, especially leaps in mathematics ([1:13:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4435s)). He mentions recent news of AI agents cracking a hard physics problem about friction in water, then solving it again faster ([1:14:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=4443s)). To him this shows reasoning, but only over what has been published.

## The idea, explained

**Founder fit.** The persona idea Salman uses is a variant of the well-known *Hacker, Hustler, Hipster* (builder, seller, designer) shorthand from startup culture. The strongest academic study we found used personality data from about 25,000 to 33,000 founders. It found that firms with three or more founders were more than twice as likely to succeed as solo founders, and that personality-diverse teams did better. Its six types are not the same as Salman's three ([McCarthy et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07968)). We have not found the source for the 40% to 55% figure.

**Psychometrics** measures traits through structured questionnaires. Big Five personality tests are the most widely used in research. They are tools for conversation and team design, not verdicts.

**AI and research, as of late 2026.** Salman's distinction holds up. In September 2026 OpenAI announced that its AI agents, running about 10,000 at a time, had resolved a version of the Navier–Stokes problem, a famous open question in fluid dynamics. The proof was confirmed by the Lean proof checker, though human review was still under way ([Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/ai-math-puzzle-navier-stokes-openai)). The claim also raised a credit dispute with human mathematicians working on a related problem. This may be the story Salman is recalling, but we have not confirmed it. Either way, the lesson matches his: AI is becoming a powerful research tool, while choosing the problem, designing the instrument and owning the result stay human.

## Interactive

**The founder triangle.** Readers place themselves and up to three co-founders on an Engineer, Hustler, Artist triangle and see where the team is thin. A note explains that this is a conversation tool, not a test.

**What AI can and can't do in research.** A two-column card: *finding and summarising literature, analysing data, checking proofs* versus *choosing the question, designing new instruments, working on unpublished problems, taking responsibility*.
