# 6. Sponsored research and the Saudi ecosystem

> "Why don't we have a private sector that pumps money into universities and tells a specialist professor: point your research in this direction?" Salman Butt, [48:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2890s)

## What Salman says

- **The Liverpool moment** ([46:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2788s)). He paid his own master's fees at Liverpool. The classmate beside him, in the same lectures, was *being paid*. The difference was **sponsored research**: a company was funding his work ([47:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2823s)).
- **Why a company pays** ([47:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2843s)). The company backs a leading professor on a problem it expects to face in two or three years. It then owns the papers and any patents, and can "own the market" when the problem arrives ([47:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2874s)).
- **What he wishes existed here** ([48:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2907s)). Companies would fund university research, then support a patentability check before publication, a study of the research's role, and *commercialisation*, turning patents into a profitable business. Rights would be shared with the researchers ([48:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2921s)).
- **KnowledgeX's aim** ([49:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2962s)). To create at least part of that ecosystem. (Asked whether he has hired 1,800 researchers yet, he laughs: "Not yet.")
- **Models abroad** ([49:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2993s)). The team studied research ecosystems in Australia and New Zealand, Europe (especially Switzerland), the UK and the US, both medical and Silicon Valley. Building one is a government-scale job, because deep-tech funding is "like a black hole": you can't know when or how much it will return ([51:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=3062s)).
- **RDIA's priorities** ([51:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=3079s)). The Research, Development and Innovation Authority set national priorities that researchers can align with. They are broad enough that most research can be fitted to one.

## The idea, explained

**Sponsored research** is a contract. A company pays a university lab to work on a question. In return it gets early access to results and usually first rights to license any resulting patents. The university keeps the right to publish, typically after a short review delay so patents can be filed first (see [chapter 4](04-research-to-patent.md)). Students funded this way get a stipend, real-world data and often a job offer.

Why it matters for an economy:

- Professors work on problems that someone will pay to solve.
- Companies get specialist talent they could not hire full-time.
- Graduates move into industry with research skills.

**What exists in Saudi Arabia today:**

- **National priorities.** On 1 July 2022 the Crown Prince announced four national R&D priorities: *health and wellness*, *sustainable environment and essential needs*, *energy and industrial leadership*, and *economies of the future*. The target is annual R&D spending of 2.5% of GDP by 2040 ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2022/07/01/saudi-arabia-outlines-new-plan-for-research-development-and-innovation)).
- **University spin-outs.** Startups from KAUST had raised more than USD 1 billion by November 2025, with combined revenue of USD 925 million ([KAUST](https://www.kaust.edu.sa/news/kaust-startups-surpass-1-billion-investments-raised)).
- **Industry bridges.** KnowledgeX positions itself here. Its Riciety platform matches private-sector challenges with researchers ([KnowledgeX](https://knowledgex.sa/)).

## Side note: how people work in research

Salman's stories show that "researcher" is not one job. Common roles in industry research:

| Role | Typical background | What they do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research scientist | PhD | Sets research questions, publishes |
| Applied scientist or research engineer | Master's or PhD, strong coding | Turns methods into working systems on real data |
| Data scientist | Any degree, strong statistics | Models business data, runs experiments |
| Research software engineer | Engineering background | Builds the tools, pipelines and platforms researchers rely on |
| IP or technology-transfer specialist | Law, engineering or science | Patentability, licensing, spin-outs |

The paper Salla's merchant benefited from needed a rainfall-model scientist *and* data scientists *and* someone to translate the results for the merchant. Most teams are mixed like this.
