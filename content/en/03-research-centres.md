# 3. Why companies build research centres

> "I want to build a company to last in the market. To last, I need knowledge assets." [22:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1337s)

## From the episode

- **His own path** ([19:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1162s)). He published papers after his master's, around 2009 to 2011. A conference then rejected one of his proposals for being "too practical, more of a project than research". That pushed him into building companies for a while.
- **Salla's research centre** ([20:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1226s)). In 2022 Salla founded a research and innovation centre, which later spun out as KnowledgeX.
- **Why the board hesitated** ([20:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1241s)). Venture investors measure returns as multiples on their investment. A research centre needs 7 to 8 years of steady spending before it pays back, at an annual return he puts at 15% to 30% ([21:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1264s)). The board saw that as too early for a startup.
- **Why companies do it anyway** ([22:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1337s)). Any product can be copied, even by your own customers while they use it. A product built on patents cannot be copied without legal risk ([23:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1380s)). That, he says, is what makes Silicon Valley companies last.
- **The German story** ([23:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1419s)). He met a researcher from a large German-language e-commerce marketplace. The researcher's job was publishing papers, for example on reordering warehouse stock by season so that in-season products sit at the front ([25:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1519s)). The research department alone had about 1,800 people, he says ([26:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1568s)).
- **"Claiming ideas"** ([27:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=1642s)). A company that wants to lead a sector claims its intellectual ground through published papers, filed patents and licensed patents. These are knowledge assets even if they do not show up in a product straight away.
- **Research is everywhere** ([41:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2503s)). Everyday rules come from studies: EU bottle caps that stay attached ([42:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2575s)), local slaughter policy in Finland ([43:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2628s)), and bidet sprays in Finnish hotels ([45:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2701s)). Inside companies too, people click "OK" on the same error for years instead of asking why it happens ([42:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t0UiPQnskk&t=2534s)).

## The idea, explained

A company has two kinds of value. One is what it *sells today*. The other is what it *knows that others don't*. A research centre turns the second kind into assets that can be owned, defended and traded:

| Asset | What it protects | How long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Published paper | Priority: proof you got there first, plus reputation | Permanently public |
| Patent | The right to stop others using the invention | About 20 years from filing in most countries |
| Trade secret | Know-how you never publish | As long as it stays secret |
| Data and models | Advantage from what only you can measure | As long as you keep collecting |

A product team earns money next quarter; a research team earns it in years. That is why startups often cut research first.

## Context and evidence

- **E-commerce companies that publish.** Zalando, Germany's largest fashion marketplace, has a science group that publishes on forecasting and replenishment. In 2025 it published a paper in *Scientific Reports* on a replenishment engine tested on about 2 million articles from about 800 merchants ([Zalando Engineering](https://engineering.zalando.com/posts/2026/01/publication-replenishment-engine.html)).
- **Bottle caps.** Under EU Directive 2019/904, single-use plastic drink containers must keep their caps attached from 3 July 2024, because caps are among the items most often found on EU beaches ([IFLScience](https://www.iflscience.com/plastic-bottle-caps-across-europe-are-changing-but-why-74393)).
